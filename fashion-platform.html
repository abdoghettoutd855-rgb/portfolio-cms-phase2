PROJECT 04 / PRODUCT VISION

AI Virtual Try-On Platform

A long-term concept for a fashion-commerce platform combining virtual try-on, merchant tools, and a path toward specialized AI.

Concept stage · Research and planning
PROJECT NOTES

This page documents the current vision and planned work. It distinguishes goals from completed implementation.

Discuss a project

Project overview

The AI Virtual Try-On concept is a planned fashion-commerce platform designed to help shoppers preview how selected garments may look on them before placing an order. The long-term vision combines a polished storefront for clothing retailers with a virtual try-on experience and, over time, a specialized AI system developed and evaluated for this task.

The problem

Online shoppers often have to judge fit, style, and appearance from product photography alone. Standard product images do not always help a customer imagine how a garment may look on their own body. Retailers also need digital experiences that distinguish their stores without making shopping more complicated.

Planned customer journey

A shopper opens a participating store, browses its catalog, chooses a garment, and provides an appropriate photo if they choose to use virtual try-on. The system then generates a preview image for review. The shopper can return to the product details and contact the store or place an order through the supported checkout or messaging flow. The experience must clearly state that a generated preview is an approximation, not a guarantee of exact fit, sizing, color, or fabric behavior.

Core product components

The intended platform has four connected components: (1) a responsive storefront for products, prices, and product details; (2) a virtual try-on workflow that accepts a person image and a garment image; (3) a merchant area for managing catalog items and orders; and (4) an AI-serving layer responsible for inference, quality checks, and operational monitoring. The first release will be scoped according to validation, budget, and technical feasibility.

Business model

The commercial direction is a multi-store SaaS platform: one core system can serve multiple clothing shops, while each shop manages its own brand, catalog, and orders. Potential pricing structures may include a setup fee, a recurring subscription, or usage-based charges for AI generation. These options will be tested with real retailers rather than assumed in advance.

Development roadmap

The roadmap is expected to move through discovery and retailer interviews, technical experiments with available research baselines, a limited prototype, evaluation of image quality and cost, storefront and merchant workflows, privacy and security reviews, and a controlled pilot. Only after the system meets defined quality, latency, reliability, and cost targets should a wider release be considered. The target is to work toward a release by the end of 2027; this is a goal, not a claim that the product is already built.

Privacy, safety, and honest expectations

Images of people are sensitive personal data in many contexts. A production design must minimize collection, explain how images are processed, set retention and deletion rules, restrict access, and obtain appropriate user consent. It must also prevent misleading product claims and make clear that virtual try-on is a visual aid rather than a reliable measurement tool. These requirements will be part of the product design, not an afterthought.

Current status

This project is currently a concept in the planning and research stage. No completed platform, proprietary trained model, or production performance results are being claimed here. The next step is to validate the retailer problem and test whether a reliable virtual try-on workflow can be built within a realistic budget.
